# PlantLens — Feature Ideas

PlantLens is a visual plant health assistant for crops, home gardens, and houseplants. Its core flow is: **photo → investigate → understand → act**.

## Core features

1. **Visual plant check**  
   Upload a photo of a leaf, stem, fruit, flower, or whole plant. PlantLens identifies visible symptoms such as yellowing, spots, curling, holes, wilting, or mold-like growth.

2. **Smart follow-up questions**  
   Ask focused questions instead of guessing from one image. Examples include whether lower leaves were affected first or whether the soil remains wet.

3. **Likely problem possibilities**  
   Present 2–4 possible causes rather than an absolute diagnosis. Causes may include watering stress, nutrient issues, pests, disease, or environmental stress.

4. **Next-action checklist**  
   Give users a short, practical list of what to inspect or do next. Suggestions can include checking leaf undersides, improving drainage, or monitoring for 48 hours.

5. **Visual symptom map**  
   Mark the image regions that appear relevant to the assessment. This makes the analysis easier to understand and more convincing in a demo.

6. **Plant history timeline**  
   Save observations, actions, and updates for each plant over time. Users can see a simple record of symptoms and recovery attempts.

7. **Progress comparison**  
   Compare a new plant photo against a previous one. Highlight whether visible symptoms appear to be improving, worsening, or changing.

8. **Care profile**  
   Store helpful context such as plant type, light, watering habits, location, soil, and recent changes. Advice becomes more relevant as the profile grows.

9. **Urgency indicator**  
   Show a clear Green / Yellow / Red status for the situation. It helps users understand whether to monitor, act soon, or isolate and inspect urgently.

10. **Explain why**  
    Let users ask why a recommendation was made. PlantLens connects the suggestion to observed symptoms and the plant’s saved context.

11. **Pest identification**  
    Suggest likely pests when insects or distinctive damage patterns are visible. Provide the next evidence to check, such as residue or insects under leaves.

12. **Watering assistant**  
    Help distinguish overwatering from underwatering using plant and soil observations. Focus on reasoning from the current situation instead of rigid schedules.

13. **Environment check**  
    Collect details on sunlight, temperature, humidity, indoor/outdoor placement, and recent weather. Combine this context with the photo assessment.

14. **Recovery plan**  
    Turn recommendations into a short plan for today, the next few days, and the following week. Include a reminder to recheck or upload another photo.

15. **Multi-plant dashboard**  
    Show all saved plants as simple status cards. Users can quickly see which plants are healthy, improving, or need attention.

16. **Ask the plant**  
    Support natural follow-up questions after an investigation. The assistant retains plant context for questions such as whether a leaf should be removed or an issue may spread.

17. **Reference-backed recommendations**  
    Link advice to the relevant plant-care or disease reference. This makes guidance more trustworthy and easier to verify.

18. **Uncertainty mode**  
    Be honest when multiple causes look similar. Ask for the most useful additional evidence, such as a close-up of the underside of a leaf.

19. **Plant rescue mode**  
    Provide a rapid guided flow for users whose plant is severely declining. Check major causes first: water, light, pests, roots, temperature, and disease.

20. **Healthy plant mode**  
    Support preventive care as well as troubleshooting. A healthy scan can confirm good signs, flag early warnings, and suggest when to check again.

## Stretch feature

21. **Live video streaming analysis** *(if time permits)*  
    Analyze a live camera stream while the user moves around the plant. Guide them toward useful angles and call out visible symptoms in real time.
